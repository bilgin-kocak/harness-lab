# Qiskit compilation on a pinned QASMBench subset: held-out result

> **DRAFT, under review.** The `reproduce.py` recompilation step is still running. Do not cite
> this file until this note is gone.

**Status: verified subset result. No improvement found.**

These are results on 26 held-out QASMBench circuits and one fixed target. They are not a
leaderboard score.

**Where the numbers come from:**
- Every held-out statistic below is in
  [`results.json`](../../studies/2026-11-quantum-qasmbench/results.json), which
  `harnesslab study report` computes from the records. The rendered tables are in
  [`tables.md`](../../studies/2026-11-quantum-qasmbench/tables.md).
- The dev-search tallies (per stage, causes of unverifiable outputs, stage 2, feasibility) and
  all times are computed from the raw records: `dev/attempts.jsonl.gz` and
  `held_out/attempts.jsonl.gz`.
- Circuit facts come from `split.json`.
- [Reproduce](#reproduce) re-derives the held-out statistics.

## Summary

- **The search.** It covered 750 Qiskit configurations: optimization level, layout, routing,
  translation, transpiler seed, approximate synthesis, and whether untouched qubits count as
  clean ancillas. Candidates ran on the 22 dev circuits whose baseline output verified; the
  baseline and level 3 ran on all 25.
- **What it found.** Nothing beat Qiskit's own optimization level 3 on dev.
  - Eight configurations tie at the top, with byte-identical outputs on all 22 circuits: ratio
    to the baseline 0.9961 for two-qubit gates and 0.9913 for depth.
  - The last pre-registered tie-break, median compile time, picked plain level 3 by 0.9 ms,
    which is timing noise. That is the level-3 comparator fixed in advance.
  - So the held-out result compares level 3 with level 2. It does not test anything new that
    the search found.
- **Held-out comparison.** On the 22 held-out circuits where both outputs verified, level 3
  (frozen) and the default, level 2 (baseline), show no detectable difference in two-qubit
  gates. 6 of 22 circuits differ: 4 favour level 3 and 2 favour level 2.

  | Two-qubit gates, frozen − baseline | Value |
  | --- | --- |
  | Mean difference | −0.05 gates per circuit [95% CI −0.64, +0.73] |
  | Geometric-mean ratio of (gates + 1) | 0.995 [0.986, 1.003] |
  | Wins / losses / ties | 4 / 2 / 16 |
  | Exact sign test | p = 0.69 |

  Depth shows no difference either: mean difference +1.2 [−1.4, +4.1], 2/3/17, p = 1.
- **With fresh seeds.** Averaged over five transpiler seeds never used in the search
  (1001–1005), the two-qubit-gate difference is −0.69 [−1.71, +0.05], 5/2/15, p = 0.45. The
  interval only just includes 0. That is not evidence that level 3 is better, and it is not
  claimed.
- **Verification.** Every outcome but six was decided by an exact simulation (pool A) or MQT
  QCEC (pool B):
  - five held-out cells on small/hhl_n7 are `unverifiable` by the tolerance-gap rule;
  - one dev cell (vqe_n24 at level 3) failed because its compiler produced no output.

  No disagreement between the checkers stopped the study. No cell was rerun. The held-out
  phase ran once and is complete: 312 of 312 cells.

## What was compared

- **System under test.** Qiskit 2.6.2 `generate_preset_pass_manager(target=TARGET, ...)`,
  compiling each circuit onto one fixed 57-qubit heavy-hex target. The target's basis is `cz`,
  `rz`, `sx` and `x`, with 128 directed coupling edges. It is recorded in `split.json` as
  `target`.
- **Baseline.** The preset pass manager at its defaults with `seed_transpiler=0`, which is
  optimization level 2. Config hash `843db842cf08`, plan B6.
  - On all 24 dev circuits checked, its outputs are identical to the plain
    `generate_preset_pass_manager(target=TARGET, seed_transpiler=0)` call (plan B14.4).
- **Frozen configuration.** `opt3`, config hash `78f2fa9955a4`: optimization level 3, every
  other knob at the baseline's value, seed 0.
  - It was chosen from dev records only: all 16,506 search cells are on the 25 dev circuits.
  - It was frozen before any held-out cell of this study ran.
  - It is the same configuration as the level-3 comparator, so the "frozen vs baseline" and
    "opt3 vs baseline" rows of `tables.md` are identical.
- **Objective (dev only, plan B7).**
  - **Feasibility.** A configuration is feasible only if its output verifies on every dev
    circuit whose baseline output verifies.
  - **Ranking.** Feasible configurations are ranked by the geometric mean, over those circuits,
    of (two-qubit gates + 1) / (the baseline's + 1).
  - **Ties.** Ties within 1e-9 go to depth, then to median compile time.
  - **Feasible count.** 274 of the 750 configurations were feasible. Of the other 476:
    - 456, all in stage 1, produced no wrong output. They are infeasible only because at least
      one of their outputs could not be checked: a pool-A output on more than 14 active
      qubits, or QCEC giving no answer on pool B. None of the 456 beats level 3 on the circuits
      it could be checked on.
    - 20 are approximate stage-2 configurations with failed outputs.

## Protocol

1. **Split by circuit.** Each pool's eligible circuits are ranked by
   `sha256(f"20261122:{id}")`, then dealt alternately to held-out and dev. This gives 51
   eligible circuits (86 skipped, with reasons, in `split.json`).
   - Pool A, at most 10 qubits: 16 dev and 16 held-out circuits.
   - Pool B, 11–27 qubits: 9 dev and 10 held-out circuits.
   - `harnesslab study check` found no held-out circuit identical to a dev circuit.
2. **Search, on dev circuits only.** Each stage's plan was written once, by exclusive create;
   its `created_at` is the stage's first cell. Each plan was then committed:
   - **`baseline`** (committed `ce0f812`, 5 minutes into the stage): the baseline and level 3
     on all 25 dev circuits.
   - **`stage1`** (committed `5f86ac1`, 3 minutes in): the grid of optimization level (0–3) ×
     layout (default, dense, sabre, trivial) × routing (default, basic, sabre) × translation
     (default, synthesis, translator) × transpiler seed (0–4).
     - That is 720 configurations, the presets themselves included.
     - They ran on the 22 dev circuits whose baseline output verified. The other three
       (vqe_n24, qft_n18 and knn_n25) have a baseline output that QCEC cannot check.
   - **`stage2`** (committed `975466b`, 28 s after its last cell and before the freeze): the 10
     best stage-1 configurations, each with approximation degree {1.0, 0.99} ×
     `qubits_initially_zero` {true, false}. That gives 40 configurations, 30 of them new.

     For this stage, git alone does not show that the plan came before its cells. The
     write-once file and its `created_at` do.
3. **Freeze.** `freeze.json` records:
   - the frozen configuration, the comparators and the robustness configurations;
   - the sealed spec sections;
   - the SHA-256 of the 16,506 dev records;
   - the adapter's provenance and the split's hash.

   The held-out phase re-checks all of these before running a cell. It found no problem
   (`audit.freeze_problems` is empty).
4. **Held-out, once.** These configurations ran on the 26 held-out circuits, 1 repetition each,
   in a seeded order: 312 cells, with no harness error and no rerun.
   - the baseline;
   - the frozen configuration (which is also the level-3 comparator);
   - the robustness configurations: the baseline and the frozen configuration with transpiler
     seeds 1001–1005.
5. **Report.** `harnesslab study report` computes `results.json` from the records. Statistics
   are on circuits where both sides verified:
   - the mean difference with a 95% paired percentile-bootstrap interval;
   - the median difference;
   - wins, losses and ties, with an exact two-sided sign test, which is also reported with
     unverified outputs counted as losses;
   - a geometric-mean ratio with its interval: of (metric + 1) for two-qubit gates and depth
     (the objective's offset), and of the metric itself for size and compile time.

   Robustness compares the per-circuit average over the five seeds.

## Verifier

Every outcome is decided by checks that are independent of the compiler (plan B8, amended by
B15):

1. **Target validity.** It checks the circuit exactly as stored (QPY version 16):
   - every operation is a standard `cz`, `rz`, `sx` or `x`, or a real barrier or delay;
   - every two-qubit gate sits on a coupling edge;
   - angles and the global phase are finite real numbers;
   - the qubits form one register in circuit order;
   - the file headers are checked before Qiskit reads the file.
2. **Equivalence up to one global phase, with layouts.** For every input state, with all other
   physical qubits starting in |0⟩, the output must reproduce the input circuit's output on the
   final-layout qubits, and return every other qubit to |0⟩.
   - **Pool A:** an exact NumPy isometry check on the active qubits, at most 14. It passes when
     fidelity ≥ 1 − 1e-9 and every amplitude is within 1e-7. QCEC also cross-checks every
     pool-A output on which the exact check decides.
   - **Pool B:** MQT QCEC 3.12.0's alternating checker, with a 60 s timeout and a 90 s hard
     kill. Only `equivalent` or `equivalent_up_to_global_phase` passes.
3. **Disagreements.** A pool-A pass/fail disagreement whose largest deviation lies between 1e-11
   and 1e-5 is explained by the two checkers' different tolerances, and the verdict is
   `unverifiable`. Any other disagreement stops the study. None happened. Five held-out cells,
   all on small/hhl_n7, were `unverifiable` by the gap rule (see
   [Failures](#failures-and-unverifiable-outputs)).
4. **Counts.** Two-qubit gates, depth and size are counted from the verified payload, not from
   the run's own record.

Two adversarial reviews attacked this verifier with real Qiskit and QCEC before the search, and
every hole they found was fixed before any search cell ran (B15).
- **Pool-B false passes**, from crafted payloads our compiler never produces:
  - integer or expression angles;
  - a register listed in reverse order;
  - a gate merely named `barrier`.
- **Crashes**, from QPY-level payloads.
- **Verdicts that changed from run to run**, from QCEC's ZX and alternating checkers racing.

**What it does not check:**
- how Qiskit's OpenQASM parser and gate definitions read the source (the reference unitary uses
  them too);
- hardware noise, calibration and timing;
- whether a circuit implements the algorithm its name suggests.

**Verification outcome (McNemar).** At seed 0, and at each of seeds 1001–1005, the baseline and
level 3 verify on the same 22 circuits and fail to verify on the same 4. With no discordant
pair, McNemar's test has nothing to detect (p = 1). The objective is a gate count, not

**Reading.**
- **No difference shown.** None is shown on the objective, overall, in either pool, or across
  seeds: every interval includes 0, and no sign test comes near significance.
- **How strong is "no difference"?** Gate counts are deterministic, and most circuits tie
  exactly, so the sign tests have little power.
  - Only 6 of 22 circuits differ in two-qubit gates. p < 0.05 would need all six to go the same
    way, which gives the smallest attainable p of 0.031.
  - In pool A (2 circuits differ) the smallest attainable p is 0.5. In pool B (4 differ) it is
    0.125.
  - The mean-difference interval is the more useful bound: −0.64 to +0.73 gates per circuit,
    about ±1% of the 59.8-gate mean.
- **Total gate count (`size`).** This was a secondary metric, not the objective. It is not
  claimed.
  - **All circuits:** −1.6 gates [−3.7, 0], 5/1/16, p = 0.22.
  - **Pool B:** level 3 has fewer gates on 4 of 7 circuits and more on none. With no losses at
    n = 7, a percentile bootstrap interval excludes 0 by construction. p = 0.125 is the smallest
    sign-test p that four non-tied circuits can give.
  - **qf21_n15:** one of those four "wins", where level 3 has 6 more two-qubit gates and 21
    more depth.

## Negative results

- **The full grid.** The full stage-1 grid (720 configurations) and the 30 stage-2 variants
  found nothing better than plain level 3 on dev. The eight configurations that tie at the top,
  level 3 included, produce byte-identical outputs on all 22 dev circuits.
- **Level 3 against level 2.** On held-out circuits, level 3 is not shown to beat level 2 on
  two-qubit gates or depth.
  - Seed 0 gives 4/2/16, p = 0.69.
  - Seeds 1001–1005 give 5/2/15, p = 0.45, with an interval [−1.71, +0.05] that only just
    includes 0.
- **Approximate synthesis.** At approximation degree 0.99, 280 of 440 dev cells failed
  verification of exact equivalence: 184 by the exact check on pool A and 96 by QCEC on pool B.
  Another 144 passed and 16 were unverifiable.
  - Each of the 20 approximate configurations failed on 10 or 15 of the 22 circuits, so none
    could be feasible under the study's exact-equivalence criterion.
  - Whether approximate outputs help under a fidelity-based criterion is not tested here.
- **`qubits_initially_zero` set to false** changed no verdict on exact outputs. All 220 such
  stage-2 cells passed, as their `true` counterparts did.

## Deviations from the plan

All of these were recorded in the plan before any search cell ran (B14, B15):
- lookahead routing and the MCX synthesis knob are not searched;
- a compile limit of 1,200 s per attempt, and an analysis limit of 1,200 s;
- the verifier hardening (B15.1–B15.11), including QCEC without the ZX checker, and no QCEC
  cross-check where it cannot change a pool-A verdict;
- the search budget raised from 8 to 48 cell-hours, after a dev-only timing probe.

  **In hindsight this raise was not needed:** the search used 6.83 cell-hours. The probe ran on
  a machine shared with other jobs, and could not benefit from the verdict cache that identical
  outputs share. The original 8 cell-hours would have covered the whole search.
- per-pool reporting via task tags.

**During the run:** the SIGKILL of vqe_n24's level-3 compile in the baseline stage, most likely
out-of-memory, described above. Nothing else.

## Limitations

- **Small subset.**
  - The result covers 22 compared circuits out of 26 held-out, and most of them tie exactly, so
    the tests have little power (see Reading).
  - It covers one target, one Qiskit version, and circuits of at most 27 qubits. Nothing from
    QASMBench's `large/` set is included.
- **Strict, checkable-only equivalence.** "Verified" is strict, for three reasons:
  - the pre-registered amplitude tolerance is 1e-7;
  - QCEC has limits of 60 s and 90 s;
  - the exact check stops at 14 active qubits.

  Consequences:
  - hhl_n7 fails or is unverifiable for every configuration, at deviations of 5e-8 to 2.5e-7.
  - Three pool-B circuits are not checkable within 90 s.
  - Excluded circuits are listed, and nothing is said about them.
  - The search could credit only configurations whose outputs are checkable: 456 configurations
    are infeasible only for that reason.
- **The held-out comparison is not the first look at this pair.** Before the split existed, a
  feasibility probe compiled and checked up to 23 of the 26 held-out circuits: the 16 pool-A
  held-out circuits, and multiply_n13, bv_n14, qf21_n15, dnn_n16, qram_n20, ghz_state_n23 and
  wstate_n27 from pool B.
  - It compiled them with level 2 and level 3 (seed 1234), the same pair compared here, and its
    level-2 against level-3 differences were known before the plan was written (plan B13).
  - It did not influence the frozen choice, which came from dev records by the pre-registered
    rule (plan, "Disclosure").
- **Split by circuit, not by family.** Four families have a circuit on each side, dev /
  held-out:
  - adder_n10 / adder_n4;
  - bv_n19 / bv_n14;
  - cat_state_n4 / cat_state_n22;
  - wstate_n3 / wstate_n27.

  A compiler configuration does not learn circuit content, but dev and held-out circuits are
  not independent families (plan B4).
- **Seeds select luck.** Choosing a transpiler seed on dev mostly selects luck. The frozen
  configuration uses seed 0; the robustness analysis gives the same conclusion across five new
  seeds.
- **Compile times are noisy.** They come from a shared 4-CPU machine, and serve only as the last
  tie-break. That tie-break picked level 3 among eight identical outputs by 0.9 ms.

## Reproduce

```bash
uv sync --all-extras
python studies/2026-11-quantum-qasmbench/reproduce.py <QASMBench checkout at 357b942> --rerun-held-out --reverify 3
```

The script checks five things, and prints PASS or FAIL for each:
1. It re-derives `split.json` from the checkout.
2. It re-checks every freeze check, except that `freeze.json` is committed: its scratch copy is
   not a git checkout. Check that one with
   `git log -- studies/2026-11-quantum-qasmbench/freeze.json`, which shows `1e5bb0b`.
3. It recomputes every held-out comparison from the committed records (`*.jsonl.gz`).
4. It checks that the held-out phase is complete.
5. It recompiles every held-out cell, and checks that each output is byte-identical to the
   recorded one. It also re-verifies 3 cells per configuration.
